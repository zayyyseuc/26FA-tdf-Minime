# 2026-11-08 Bodystorm 1 — recruiter scenario

Setup: Zia plays the host on her own website; Claude plays visitors. Primary visitors: people who know nothing about her (admissions, recruiters, practitioners).

## Scenario 1: recruiter, in a hurry
Visitor: "Hi. What do you do?"

Zia's moves (her words, paraphrased):
1. Opens with: she does design research in HCI / interaction design. (Note: not environmental design, not elderly care. Deliberate choice for strangers, or not yet decided how to say 医养? -> open question)
2. Asks: what is your business area? Reason: she has design methods but lacks practice in specific business domains.
3. Wants the visitor to come back with useful business information. The visitor is a source of information for her, not only an audience.

Visitor reply: "B2B logistics software, dashboards for dispatchers. Most of our designers come from product design."

Zia's next moves:
1. Restates her understanding of their business in neutral, plain language, and asks "is that right?" (no jargon).
2. Then offers which of her skills could apply to what they said (e.g. UX / experience research).
3. Mini-Me should record the visitor's business description **verbatim**, then re-analyze the skills it implies **using the visitor's own words**, and hand this to Zia.

## Design rules extracted
- Restate-then-confirm before offering anything (Pask: agree on meaning before acting).
- Skills offered are mapped to what the visitor said, not a generic list.
- Verbatim capture is deterministic (store as-is). Skill analysis is probabilistic and must quote the visitor.
- Handoff to Zia = visitor's verbatim business description + skills-needed analysis.

## Open questions
- Does Mini-Me state the experience gap (no logistics/B2B) out loud? Zia did not say. Must not claim experience she lacks.
- Which skills may Mini-Me claim on her behalf? Needs a human-written skills list; the model must not invent.
- Is the visitor told their words are saved for Zia? (visible vs invisible)
- HCI/interaction framing vs elderly care framing for strangers.

## Scenario 2: elderly-care practitioner asks for an opinion
Visitor: "Do you think AI can actually help with elderly care?"

Zia (paraphrased):
1. Would answer "yes" and then give her own explanation (explanation not yet captured).
2. Mini-Me can handle almost all online Q&A in her place. Hand off to the real Zia only when the visitor explicitly asks to contact her. What she needs: the visitor should feel this AI *is* her.
3. Does NOT keep throwing questions back at the visitor. Only asks when the visitor raises something absent from her knowledge base (project experience, daily thinking).

## Design rules extracted (2)
- Default: answer in first person from the knowledge base. Handoff is visitor-triggered, not model-triggered.
- Asking back is purposeful: only to fill a gap (her business-domain gap in S1; KB gap in S2). Not a conversational habit.
- Success criterion = "feels like her" (voice + way of thinking), the brief's baseline test.

## Open questions (2)
- Tension with earlier rule "AI should not decide opinions/commitments": she now delegates nearly all of it. Where is the hard line, if any?
- First-person answers + "feels like her" => biggest risk is the model claiming experience she doesn't have. Needs grounding rule + eval.
- Her actual explanation of "yes": does it keep the unresolved tension (AI can't enter touch/taste/embodied care), or flatten it?
- Disclosure: should the visitor know it's an AI agent? (feels like her != pretends to be human her)

## Scenario 3: asked about experience that is not in the knowledge base
Visitor (elderly-care design recruiter): "Have you done user research with elderly residents? What did you learn from talking to caregivers?"

Zia: Must sound like her, must not fabricate. Answer: "I haven't done this specifically on this topic, but here are the user research I have done, and the reusable methodology from it." No ask-back in this case.

## Design rules extracted (3)
- Gap pattern: say plainly "not specifically this" -> name the closest REAL work -> give the transferable method. Never invent.
- No ask-back when the gap is about her experience (ask-back is for filling HER information gap, see S1).
- Corpus implication: each project needs an "evidence card" (what research was actually done, how, what method is reusable). The model can only pivot to what is written down.

## Still missing from Zia
- Her explanation of why AI can help elderly care (S2)
- List of real user research she has done + methods
- Skills list she is willing to claim
- Decision on AI disclosure label
